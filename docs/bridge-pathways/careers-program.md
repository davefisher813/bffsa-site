THE BRIDGE FOUNDATION FOR STUDENT ATHLETES

## Bridge Pathways: Careers Program
The complete Careers program materials: the program document and the advisor list. The Careers application is its own document, Bridge Pathways: Careers Application. Finance stays exactly as it is. Careers is the new side of Pathways, and Trades sits under it.
What Careers is, in one line: a free, one to one program that connects a Bridge athlete or alumnus with a real advisor in the field he is interested in, checks that the interest is the right fit first, and then helps him take real steps toward that work.
Everything in Pathways is free to participants. Bridge connects and prepares. Bridge never guarantees a job, an interview outcome, or a wage. The employer hires and decides.

### Part 1: Pathways has two sides
Bridge Pathways helps Bridge athletes and alumni build a real life after sport. It has two sides.

#### Finance
Finance teaches money skills: how money works, how to handle it, and how to make sound decisions with it. Finance keeps its own documents and stays exactly as it is. Nothing in this remodel changes Finance, its sessions, or its rules.

#### Careers
Careers helps an athlete or alumnus move toward real work in a field he is genuinely interested in. It is not a job board and it is not a placement promise. It is a personal connection to people who do the work, honest guidance about whether the field fits him, and steady help taking the next steps.
Trades sits under Careers. The trades work Bridge already runs, with its licensed partners, apprenticeships, and placements, continues as the Trades branch of Careers.

#### Who Careers serves
Current Bridge athletes, including high school athletes.
Bridge athletes in college, with advising while they are in school and help toward internships and entry level work.
Bridge alumni, with real assistance once they graduate and beyond.
Careers is run by Dave Fisher for now. It runs one to one: every athlete is matched around his own interest, not placed into a group program.

### The six categories
An athlete names one category on his application: the field he wants to explore. Each category below is in the program because Bridge has real people it can connect him with.
Trades. Skilled trades such as plumbing, electrical work, construction, and auto body. This branch is already built, with named partners and both an apprenticeship path for high school athletes and a placement path for graduates.
Sports and Fitness. Coaching, training, facility operations, and the fitness business. Health related interests are served on the fitness side of this category.
Business. Working inside a business: marketing, finance, sales, operations, and office roles.
Entrepreneurship. Building a business of your own. This stands apart from Business on purpose: working in a business and building one are different paths.
Media. Content, production, broadcasting, and the creative side of sports and business.
Public Service. Work that serves the public, such as local government, public safety, education, and community organizations.
What is not on the list, and why: there is no Tech category. Bridge does not have a real tech person behind it today, and the program does not promise a connection it cannot make. If that changes, the category gets added with a real name behind it.

### How Careers works, step by step
He applies and names his area. He completes the Careers application, naming the one category he is interested in and saying in his own words why it interests him.
He has the evaluation conversation. Before any other work starts, he sits down for a conversation with his potential advisor in that category. This conversation is required. The next section covers what it is and what it is not.
The one to one work begins. If the fit is right, he and his advisor set real next steps for his situation: learning what the work takes, meeting people in the field, preparing a resume, getting ready for applications or job interviews, and pursuing internships, apprenticeships, or entry level work as his stage allows.
Bridge stays in contact. Bridge checks in with both the athlete and the advisor as the work goes on, helps with problems that come up, and helps cover real costs on the path when a cost is more than the athlete can afford.
If the evaluation conversation shows the fit is not right, that is a good outcome too. He learns it early, honestly, and he can name a different category and start again with a different advisor.

### The evaluation conversation
The conversation with the potential advisor is a required step in Careers. It happens after the application and before the one to one work starts.

#### What it is
It is a conversation, not an interview. Nobody is hiring, and nobody is being tested for a job. The advisor and the athlete talk, just the two of them, about the interest itself:
Is this field the right fit for him?
Is this the right profession for what he wants and how he works?
Does he have the wrong idea about what the work actually is day to day?
What would his first real steps look like if the fit is right?

#### Why it comes first
A lot of young men pick a field from the outside of it. The conversation puts him in front of someone who does the work before he spends time, or Bridge spends resources, going the wrong way. It protects the athlete from a wrong path and protects the advisor from being handed someone whose interest has never been checked against the reality of the field.

#### What comes out of it
One of two things. Either the fit is right and the one to one work begins with that advisor, or the fit is not right and the athlete and Bridge talk about a different category or a different next step. Both outcomes count as the program working.
These advisors already know and talk with Bridge athletes in the ordinary course of the program. The evaluation conversation is an ordinary advisor conversation, held one to one.

### Financial support, and plain promises

#### The program is free, and Bridge helps with real costs
There are no fees to participants in Pathways, ever. Beyond that, Careers supports its athletes financially. Where a real cost on the path is more than an athlete can afford, Bridge helps cover it.

#### What Bridge does
Connects the athlete with real people in the field he names.
Prepares him: honest guidance, readiness, and help presenting himself well.
Stays in contact with him and his advisor as the work goes on.
Helps cover real costs on the path when he cannot afford them.

#### What Bridge does not do
Bridge does not guarantee a job, an internship, an apprenticeship, an interview outcome, or a wage.
The employer hires, pays, supervises, and decides. Anyone placed works for the employer, under the rules of that employer.
Careers guidance is guidance. It is not licensed counseling, and nothing in Pathways is personal financial advice.
For athletes under 18, a parent or guardian signs the application first, and any work follows what is legal and age appropriate.

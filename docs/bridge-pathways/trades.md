## Bridge to the Trades: The Careers Branch
College is one path. This is another. Bridge to the Trades is the Trades branch of Bridge Pathways: Careers, connecting Bridge athletes and alumni who want to work with their hands with licensed trade professionals.
What this branch is, in one line: a free path to a real apprenticeship or a real job, where Bridge makes the connection, helps him get ready, and the trade partner hires and decides.
Bridge connects and prepares. Bridge never guarantees a job, an interview outcome, or a wage. The trade partner hires and decides.

### A real job, not a program
Bridge to the Trades connects Bridge Foundation athletes and alumni with licensed trade professionals for apprenticeships and job placement. Bridge finds the young people who want to work with their hands, makes sure they are serious, and connects them with a professional in the field. From there, the professional runs their own process on their own terms.
Anyone placed through Bridge to the Trades is an employee of the trade partner, under that partner's rules. Bridge does not run the jobsite.

#### Where Trades sits
Bridge Pathways has two sides. Finance teaches money skills, and it stays exactly as it is. Careers helps an athlete or alumnus move toward real work in the field he is interested in. Trades sits under Careers as one of its six categories, and it is the branch that is already built.
Careers runs one to one, built around each guy's own interest, and Dave Fisher runs it for now. Trades works the same way: one guy, one trade, one real connection.

### Who it is for: two groups

#### College graduates
Guys in their twenties who finished school and need a real job. For them this is placement. The trade partner either hires them directly, or advises them on the real path into the trade: school, license, union, another shop, whatever the path actually is. Even advising alone is worth more to them than what they have right now.

#### High school athletes
Still in school or just out. For them this is an apprenticeship, shaped however the trade partner wants: shadowing, summer work, weekends. Whatever is realistic and legal at their age. A sixteen year old is not showing up on a jobsite, and the apprenticeship is built around that fact.
All ages are in. Careers serves current Bridge athletes, including high school athletes, athletes in college, and alumni. The shape of the work changes with his age and stage. The standard does not: he has to be serious, and he has to show up.

### How it works
He applies and names Trades. He completes the Careers application, which is its own document, and names Trades as his category, saying in his own words why the work interests him.
He has the evaluation conversation. Before any other work starts, he sits down for a conversation with his potential advisor in the trade. It is required. It is a conversation, not an interview: is this trade the right fit, is it the right profession for him, and does he have the wrong idea about what the work actually is day to day? Just the advisor and the athlete talk.
Bridge checks that he is serious. Bridge reviews the application and the outcome of the conversation, and filters out anyone who is not serious before a partner's time is spent.
Bridge makes the connection. Bridge sends his name to the trade partner in the trade he named.
The partner screens and decides. The partner screens him however they want, on whatever terms they want, and decides who they take.
Applying does not get you a job. The trade partner runs its own hiring and decides. If the evaluation conversation shows the trade is not the right fit, he learns it early and honestly, and he can name a different Careers category and start again.

### What comes with it
A direct connection to a licensed professional in the trade you want
A real shot at an apprenticeship or a job, not a brochure
Honest guidance on what it actually takes to get into the trade
Boots, tools, and certifications covered by Bridge if you cannot afford them
Someone at Bridge checking in on you the whole time, so problems come to us first
Wages and the day to day work are set by each trade partner, on that partner's terms.

#### What is expected of you
Show up. Every time. On time.
Take it seriously. This is someone's business and your reputation.
Listen more than you talk, especially at the start.
If something is wrong, tell Bridge. Problems come to us first.

### Free, with real support behind it
There is no cost to apply and no cost to participate. If you cannot afford the boots, the tools, or a certification, Bridge covers it. Careers supports athletes financially in every category: where a real cost on the path is more than a guy can afford, Bridge helps cover it.

### For parents: safety first
Your child's safety comes first. All placements for participants under 18 require signed parental consent. Apprenticeship activities for minors are limited to what is legal and age appropriate. Bridge stays in contact with every placed participant for the duration of the program.
Pending legal review: liability, insurance, and parental consent terms for minors on contractor jobsites are under review before any minor is placed.

### How to apply
Complete the Careers application. It is a separate document from this one. Name Trades as your category.
Have the evaluation conversation with your potential advisor in the trade. It is required, and it comes before any placement work.
Bridge reviews and connects you with the trade partner if you are a fit.
The trade partner screens you and decides.
A guy can be in both sides of Pathways. Finance and Careers each have their own documents under one Pathways umbrella, and taking part in one does not close the other.
